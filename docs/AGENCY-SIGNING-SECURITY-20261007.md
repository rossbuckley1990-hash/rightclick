## Next trust invariant remains RED

Production G9 is incomplete. The current bare-pin verifier and five-field envelope cannot enforce a managed issuer/key identity, key validity interval, trusted runtime identity, trust-root rotation, or revocation policy across historical/live receipts. Local current-file/reference revalidation does not provide distributed or atomic broker revocation. A delete-and-restore or change-and-restore between filesystem checks may evade observation; the host owner/kernel remains trusted. Existing already-issued receipts can still be mathematically valid under a historical pin after local withdrawal; that is not evidence that a current trust policy accepts them.

The next generic primitive should be a host-owned receipt trust policy for managed issuer/key records and authenticated runtime identity, consulted by every independent verifier. Its policy must explicitly define live versus historical receipt acceptance, key expiry/revocation, rotation continuity and task binding. That needs a real legal rotation/expiry/revocation campaign, not a new bespoke substrate receipt format or a key ID added silently to version 1. Privacy-minimized receipts and broker-wide credential secrecy also remain RED. Native Windows account/tunnel cleanup, full caller authentication, issuer broker, cross-host hierarchy and the complete agency profile were not demonstrated by this review.
