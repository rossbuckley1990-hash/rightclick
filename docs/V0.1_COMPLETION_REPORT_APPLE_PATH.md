## Proven versus hypothesis

Proven: useful native app capabilities can appear automatically after ordinary installation and can be executed generically without a bespoke AI integration for that app. BBEdit proves acquisition plus an exact semantic result. Yojam supplies a second independent acquisition example and demonstrates a reliability boundary: declared compatibility and accepted invocation do not guarantee the intended external outcome.

Not proven: universal application coverage, broad cross-provider semantic reliability, durable unattended automation, customer demand, willingness to pay, or company economics. MCP remains useful transport; these results do not show it disappears.

The evidence **strengthens the bounded technical feasibility of Capability Reflection**, while weakening any claim that reflection alone makes provider execution reliable. It leaves commercial/company viability unproven. The truthful smallest v0.1 can launch on the BBEdit proof once the external distribution gates pass.
